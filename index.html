I am a computer science graduate from LUMS, where I am advised by Professor Fareed Zaffar and Dr Ashish Gehani.

My research focuses on the security of autonomous LLM agents. I study whether autonomous LLM agents can be trusted, develop methods to detect compromised or malicious agents, and build evaluation frameworks that test whether claims about LLM behavior actually hold up.

I am currently working with Dr. Ashish Gehani, Dr. Ramneet Kaur and Eric Yeh at SRI International, and with Professor Yasir Zaki at NYU Abu Dhabi.

Publications

  1. Understanding or Imitation? Auditing Conceptual Understanding and Reasoning in Large Language Models M. S. Hassan, E. Jan, R. Kaur, E. Yeh, F. Zaffar, A. Gehani ACM Conference on Reproducibility and Replicability (REP), 2026 pdf·code The "Potemkin Understanding in LLMs" paper argues that benchmarks credit LLMs with conceptual understanding they do not actually possess. In this work, we reproduced both of its evaluation techniques and extended our analysis to include both smaller and reasoning models. While we found that the original directional claims do stand, the reported scores are highly unstable varying by up to 31% across identical runs. We also observed that reasoning models significantly reduce this incoherence suggesting that these failures are driven by the inference regime rather than the underlying model itself.
  2. Data Doping or True Intelligence? Evaluating the Transferability of Injected Knowledge in LLMs E. Jan, M. Ali, M. S. Hassan, Y. Zaki, M. F. Zaffar Findings of EMNLP, 2025 pdf·acl anthology·arxiv We injected new knowledge into five model families and tested whether the models could genuinely use it beyond the task they were finetuned on or only recite it as it was given. We found that comprehension-style tasks led to substantially greater knowledge transfer than surface-level mappings such as translation.
    In a nutshell, how you teach a model matters as much as what you teach it.

In preparation

  1. Compromised by Context: Systematizing Prompt Injection Against Autonomous LLM Agents in preparation · targeting IEEE S&P 2027 An agent that browses the web will read whatever the web puts in front of it, and some of that text is written specifically to hijack it. I am mapping the full space of these attacks and the defenses proposed against them to work out which defenses actually hold and which only look strong because nobody has pushed on them properly yet.
  2. Escalate, Don’t Improvise: Comparability in Autonomous Measurement Fleets (AmiClaw) in preparation · targeting HotMobile 2027 Measurement studies are still run by hand: a researcher designs the experiment, babysits the runs, and stitches the data together. I am building fleets of autonomous agents that run these studies themselves. The catch is that improvising agents could quietly produce incomparable results so I argue an agent that hits ambiguity should escalate it to a fixed protocol instead of improvising around it on its own.
  3. SENTRY: Adversarial Agent Detection in Open Multi-Agent Systems in progress In an open system where agents from different owners interact, some of them will be malicious, and nothing about an agent’s label tells you which. SENTRY asks whether honest agents can tell an adversarial peer apart from its behavior alone, early enough to matter.
